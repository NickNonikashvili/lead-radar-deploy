§5.1 Constructing accurate graphs of antiderivatives

Study notes for Constructing accurate graphs of antiderivatives in M171 Calculus I at Montana State University: the big ideas, key formulas, a worked example, common mistakes and an exam tip. The same topic has endless practice questions and flashcards in MatHub.

Big ideas

  • If $F' = f$, then $F$ is increasing where $f>0$, decreasing where $f<0$, has local extrema where $f$ changes sign, and is concave up where $f$ is increasing.
  • The integral function $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt$ is the antiderivative of $f$ with $F(a)=0$; its values are net signed areas.
  • The indefinite integral $\int f(x)\,dx = F(x) + C$ is the whole family of antiderivatives.

Key formulas

Integral function
$$F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt,\qquad F(a)=0,\ F'(x) = f(x)$$
Indefinite integral
$$\int f(x)\,dx = F(x) + C$$

Worked example

$f$ is positive on $(0,2)$, zero at $2$, negative on $(2,4)$. Describe $F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt$ on $[0,4]$.
Show the solution
$F(0)=0$; $F$ increases on $(0,2)$ to a local (here absolute) maximum at $x=2$, then decreases on $(2,4)$. Its maximum value is the area under $f$ on $[0,2]$.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting + C on an indefinite integral.
  • Confusing where f is zero (F has extrema) with where f has extrema (F has inflection points).
Exam tipGraphing F from f is exactly the reverse of graphing f′ from f in §1.4. Same sign chart, read the other way.
