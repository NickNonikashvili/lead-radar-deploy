§4.1 Determining distance traveled from velocity

Study notes for Determining distance traveled from velocity in M171 Calculus I at Montana State University: the big ideas, key formulas, a worked example, common mistakes and an exam tip. The same topic has endless practice questions and flashcards in Mathub.

Big ideas

  • If velocity $v(t)\ge0$, the distance traveled on $[a,b]$ is the area under the velocity graph. For simple shapes (rectangles, triangles, trapezoids) compute geometrically.
  • When $v$ changes sign, area above the axis counts positive and below counts negative for net change in position; total distance adds the absolute areas.
  • Approximate irregular areas with rectangles: this is the seed of the Riemann sum.
  • Position is an antiderivative of velocity: if $s'=v$ then $s(b) - s(a) = $ net signed area under $v$.

Key formulas

Net change in position
$$s(b)-s(a) = \int_a^b v(t)\,dt$$
Total distance
$$D = \int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt$$

Worked example

$v(t) = t - 2$ m/s on $[0,4]$. Find the net change in position and the total distance.
Show the solution
The graph is a line crossing zero at $t=2$: a triangle of area $2$ below the axis and a triangle of area $2$ above. Net change $= -2 + 2 = 0$ m; total distance $= 2 + 2 = 4$ m.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting net change when total distance is asked.
Exam tipSketch the velocity graph first; most 4.1 problems are areas of triangles and rectangles.
