What's new in StudyCat

Every release, newest first. StudyCat ships most weeks; the current build is 2026.10.30.6. Ideas and bug reports are welcome on the class board or with the flag on any page.

Calmer screens, and the genie only when you close a study page

Fri, Oct 30, 2026 · build 2026.10.30.6
  • The top of the screen is quieter: your next exam is plain words with a small red dot, new notifications show as a dot, Call Bo is an outlined button, and your streak is just the number. Each page keeps one bold button, its own.
  • Your home and each class’s exam week are calmer too: classes show as a coloured dot beside their code, the days to an exam are one big number, and the rows are split by thin lines instead of coloured boxes. The last purple gradients are gone.
  • The window-into-the-sidebar animation now plays only when you close a study page (a lesson, flashcards, a quiz, a tool) and go back to the dashboard or another page that is not for studying. Every other page change is instant.

Pages go into the sidebar like a window into the Dock

Fri, Oct 30, 2026 · build 2026.10.30.5
  • When you change page, the one you leave now closes the way a window goes into the Dock on a Mac. It bends into a funnel with curved sides, gets squeezed thinner and thinner, and is pulled into the sidebar right at the link for that page. The text stays sharp the whole way.
  • The sidebar’s edge stretches out where the page goes in, then snaps back with a little bounce. On a phone the page goes up into the menu button, which squashes and springs back.
  • It takes about half a second, the new page is ready underneath straight away, and nothing moves if you turn on Reduce motion.

Pages close like a Mac window, and open before you click

Fri, Oct 30, 2026 · build 2026.10.30.4
  • Changing page now closes the old one like a window on a Mac: it bends into a funnel and is pulled down into the bottom of the screen, while the new page settles in. Going Back opens the page up out of the funnel again. It is quicker than before, about a third of a second.
  • Pages open faster: when your pointer rests on a link to a class (or your finger lands on one), StudyCat starts loading that class straight away, so it is usually ready by the time you click. The same goes for pages that load the first time you open them, like What’s new.
  • Paragraphs no longer end on a single word on its own line, headings wrap evenly, and numbers that change (the clock, countdowns, points) no longer make the text beside them shift.

Pages that bubble away

Fri, Oct 30, 2026 · build 2026.10.30.3
  • Opening a page now turns the one you were on into a rounded bubble that sinks below the screen, while the new page settles in underneath. Going Back brings the page up again from below, and it opens out to fill the screen. It is quick: under half a second.
  • The sidebar, the top bar and Ask Bo stay where they are; only the page moves. On a phone, picking something from the menu slides the menu shut as the page changes.
  • If you turn on Reduce motion (in Customize or on your device), pages simply change, as before.

Back takes you back where you were

Fri, Oct 30, 2026 · build 2026.10.30.2
  • Going Back (or Forward) now puts you where you were on that page, not at the top: open a lesson from a long list, come back, and you are still at that spot. Reloading a page keeps your place too. A new page still starts at the top.
  • A button that has to wait for the server (saving, sending, joining) now shows a small spinner if it takes a moment, and a double tap sends it only once.
  • The daily word shows your result and Share once the last tiles have turned over, and a card you hide pops into Add to dashboard so you can see where it went.

Small things that feel better

Fri, Oct 30, 2026 · build 2026.10.30.1
  • Hid a card by mistake? The note that says it is hidden now has Undo, and the card goes back where it was.
  • Buttons give a little when you press them, menus open from their ⋮ and fade away when you close them, and a card you hide fades out before the others slide over to fill its place.
  • The daily word turns its tiles over one at a time when you send a try, each letter pops in as you type it, and a solved row gives a little hop.
  • A ticked box on the exam checklist gives a small bounce, and the steps on the welcome page fade in as you switch between them.
  • Screen readers now hear the short notes that appear at the bottom of the screen (Saved, Copied, Hidden). They also stay put while your pointer or keyboard is on them.
  • Everything that moves stays still if you turn on Reduce motion, in Customize or on your device.

Share your daily word

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.9
  • After the daily word, Share your result posts how many tries it took and a row of coloured squares for each try, with no letters, so nothing is given away to friends who have not played yet.
  • On a phone it opens the share sheet; on a computer it is copied, ready to paste in your group chat. The link in it is your invite link: a classmate who signs up through it gets Bo credits, and so do you.

A new look, a new home and a daily word

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.8
  • StudyCat has a new design: a calm navy at night, its bright twin by day (it follows your device, and the light-or-dark button still switches), flat cards and new headings.
  • Your home now says one thing at the top: your next exam and how ready you are, or the class you studied last and how it went. Below it are cards for today: your plan, your week, your classes, flashcards, mistakes and discussions. Hide any card from its ⋮ menu and bring it back from Add to dashboard, where the tools wait too. Arrange freely still lets you drag everything anywhere.
  • The daily word: guess a five-letter word from your classes in six tries, for 20 XP and a streak. After, you see what it means in that class.
  • Teach a class? The new Learning or teaching switch at the top of the sidebar opens StudyCat Faculty in the same frame, with a home of its own: your next class, how the class is doing, what is coming up, attendance and your syllabi.
  • A first visit now gets a proper welcome page with its own header, the three steps as tabs and an example question to try.
  • Fixed: a checklist tick or a flashcard you marked could be lost the first time, if nothing had been saved for that class before.
  • Fixed: a setting changed in the last seconds before you reloaded or closed the page (hiding a card, picking a theme) could come back the old way. It is now sent as the page closes.

Quicker to tap

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.7
  • Answering a practice question is about twice as quick to show its result on a phone: the page now redraws only that question and the score, not the whole set and all its maths.
  • Bo’s little “Missed one?” tip by the corner no longer freezes the page for a moment a few seconds after you answer. It now decides from a quick glance instead of reading the whole page.
  • Confetti and the “+XP” float cost your phone much less, so the next tap after a celebration is not slowed down.

Flashcards that come back at the right time

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.6
  • Flashcards now space themselves out, like the best study apps: a card you know comes back after a day, then 3 days, a week, 16 days and 35 days, just before you would forget it. The Flashcards page and Today use the same schedule.
  • The page shows the cards due now, and the button tells you what it will do (“Got it · in 3 days”). When nothing is due it says so, and when the next card comes back. You can still go through every card any time.
  • A card you miss comes back a few cards later in the same sitting. Going through a deck twice in one sitting no longer counts as mastering it: a card moves up only when it was due.

You always know it’s saved

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.5
  • When you go offline, a short line at the bottom of the screen says so, and that what you do is kept on this device. When you are back, it says “Back online · syncing…” and then “everything is synced”, and goes away.
  • Coming back online now sends what waited right away, for your classes and your planner. Before, it could wait until your next change or the next visit. On a patchy connection that never says it is offline, StudyCat keeps trying on its own every half minute or so.
  • Getting back online no longer reloads the page under you while you are typing, and no longer adds an extra background check each time.

Lighter to open, calmer while you study

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.4
  • Pages open faster: the planner and personal pages (tasks, timetable, calendar, time tracker, projects, habits, goals, wheel of life, journal, money, career, portfolio), the notebook, peer review, the study room, GPA, the class tools and drafts now load the first time you open one, not with every page. They are saved in the background afterwards, so they still open offline.
  • Less work for your phone while you read: Bo’s head bob, the floating Bo button and the pixel sprites move a few times when they appear and then rest, and Bo still blinks now and then. Before, they kept the browser redrawing the page many times a second even when nothing changed, which costs battery. Motion that stops by itself is also easier on anyone who finds it distracting.
  • Scrolling stutters less on slower phones: the cards and bars that slide in as you scroll measure the page once and then move, instead of measuring between every move.
  • Quicker as StudyCat grows: signing in, your list of signed-in devices, the calendar feed, invite links and Bo’s call minutes now go straight to their records instead of reading through every one.
  • If StudyCat ever reaches its budget for Bo for a day, Bo rests until the next day and says so. None of your credits are used while he rests, and everything else works as usual.
  • The terms now say how to report a copyright problem (section 1.16).

Thu, Oct 29, 2026 · build 2026.10.29.3

  • The study guides move too: the header settles as you scroll, a reading line shows how far you are in a topic, parts lift in as you reach them, and the worked solution eases open.
  • Escape now closes the sign-in window wherever you are in it, and the form keeps your place while it checks.

Mathub is now StudyCat: your own classes, real lessons and tools for every class

Wed, Oct 28, 2026 · build 2026.10.28.2
  • Mathub has a new name: StudyCat, at studycat.org. Old links to mathub.space open the same page here, share codes included. Sign in once on the new address: your classes, progress and settings are with your account.
  • Every class is now your own. Upload or paste your syllabus and Bo reads it (with credits) and opens the class maker with everything filled in: the name, instructor and office hours, meeting days, exams, grading and topics. Change anything, then save; making and editing classes is free, any time, from Class maker in the sidebar. Bo can also write the lessons for each unit.
  • You pick your classes. StudyCat no longer adds every Montana State class for you: the home page asks which ones you take, nothing ticked, and only those show in the sidebar, on your home page and in search. Change them any time in Settings. Classes you make yourself are always yours.
  • Montana State classes are yours to set up too: after you pick them, StudyCat asks you to make them yours: your section, instructor, days, room, colour, exam dates, grading weights and your own dates on the calendar. It is saved with your account.
  • A home page you arrange yourself. Drag widgets anywhere, resize them, let them overlap and bring one to the front; add or remove widgets (your next exam, today, Bo, your classes, your week, what is due, a clock, a timer, your to-do list and more). On a phone they stack. The learning path is inside each class now.
  • Section notes are lessons. Every topic of all 13 classes starts with the idea in plain words, then a picture (a graph, a diagram or a table), the key points, worked examples, the usual mistakes and a short self-check with answers you can reveal. Classes Bo writes from your syllabus get the same format. On a phone the lesson comes first and the list of topics after it.
  • Every class has a Tools page (in its menu, under Explore), with the tools that class needs. Calculus: Riemann sums, a limit table and an equation solver. Physics: a formula solver for kinematics, forces and energy, the constants and a best-fit line for lab data. Biology: a Punnett square, a chi-square test and Hardy–Weinberg. Writing: revision and peer-review checklists, citations and a word counter. Japanese culture and International relations: a timeline you can be quizzed on, and game theory. Trigonometry: the unit circle and a triangle solver. Engineering: a decision matrix and stress formulas. And more.
  • The formula solver finds any letter: fill in what you know, leave one box empty, and it works out that one (the time a ball lands, an angle from three sides). Change a class’s tools for yourself with Change tools: hide one, add another, or add a formula of your own.
  • Classes you make get tools too: Bo suggests them when he reads your syllabus, a class without them gets the tools of its subject (chemistry gets the gas laws, sig figs and units), and the class maker has a Tools tab to choose them.
  • Retired: Request a class and classes published for everyone. A class you make is yours alone until you share its link with a classmate, who gets their own copy.

No tracking, in writing

Tue, Oct 27, 2026 · build 2026.10.27.2
  • StudyCat has never had ads, analytics or tracking pixels, and now the privacy policy says so plainly: no Meta, Google or TikTok pixels, no session recording, no third-party cookies, and no open-tracking in emails. The only cookie is the one that keeps you signed in.
  • The pages now refuse pictures from other websites as well as scripts, so a tracking pixel could not be added by mistake, and the newsletter no longer loads its logo from the site when you open it. The policy also names the dictionary service the Phonetics Lab asks when you look up a word.

Safer backups, and a better-run StudyCat

Mon, Oct 26, 2026 · build 2026.10.26.3
  • Backups off the server: every day’s copy of StudyCat is now encrypted and also kept with a separate storage provider, and once a week one is restored as a test, so a lost server does not mean lost work. No copy is kept longer than 90 days.
  • Bo is tested: a set of real questions, with what a good answer must and must not say, is put to him whenever something changes, so a change that makes him worse is caught before you see it.
  • Class requests are now read most wanted first, so the classes the most students ask for come first.

Check my work, notes from slides, peer review, and a page for instructors

Mon, Oct 26, 2026 · build 2026.10.26.2
  • Check my work: take a photo of your work on one problem (in a math or science class) and Bo finds the first line that goes wrong, says why and gives you a hint. StudyCat checks the arithmetic itself, so the line marked is the one that really goes wrong. He never writes the answer, and the photo is not kept.
  • Notes from slides: open your lecture slides (PowerPoint or PDF) and Bo turns them into study notes and flashcards from what the slides say. Flip the cards with a tap or the arrow keys, download the notes as a Word file and the cards for Anki or Quizlet. The slides are read on your device; only their text goes to Bo.
  • Office-hours prep: Bo picks three questions worth asking at office hours from the questions you missed lately in the class, or from what you say you are stuck on, with what to bring.
  • Peer review swaps, when your instructor turns them on: offer a draft and you are paired with a classmate working on the same project. You each read the other’s draft, comment on the words you select and write a note on the whole draft. What they send shows on your review, and goes to Bo with your next version.
  • Share a version of a draft with your instructor, with a note: they see it with Bo’s feedback and can reply. You can stop sharing any time.
  • For instructors: each class has an Instructor page for the people who teach it. Set Bo to give hints only, add notes he reads in every chat and call, give him each project’s assignment sheet and rubric, read the drafts students share with you, see patterns across the class (only once five students let their drafts count, never names or words), and give your students credits. StudyCat Faculty syllabi can now list papers and a writer’s notebook. Read more on the new For instructors page.
  • Translated into all seven languages, along with a few words on the class dashboard that were still in English.

Revision history, a writer’s notebook, and drafts read aloud

Mon, Oct 26, 2026 · build 2026.10.26.1
  • Answer Bo’s notes on your draft: press Done, or I disagree and say why. Your next draft’s review reads your answers: Bo drops a point when your reason holds, and only counts a note as done when the draft shows it.
  • Ask Bo about this, under any note, opens Bo on that note with “What do you mean here?” ready to send. In a writing class he explains, points to examples from the readings and asks questions; he still never writes your sentences.
  • Read it aloud: hear your draft sentence by sentence, marked as it goes, at the speed you pick. Hearing it is the easiest way to catch what reads oddly. No credits; it uses your device’s own voice.
  • Revision history for each project: every version you shared, any two compared side by side or in one text with the added and taken-out words marked, what became of each of Bo’s biggest revisions, questions for your portfolio reflection, and the whole thing as a Word file.
  • Open a PDF of your draft, not only a Word file: StudyCat reads its text in your browser, paragraphs and all, and leaves out page numbers and running heads.
  • WRIT 101’s writer’s notebook: write all fifteen entries on StudyCat, saved as you type and kept with your account. This week’s entries are marked, and the whole notebook downloads as one double-spaced Word file for handing in. Private, with no AI. Written offline or on two devices? Nothing is lost: if both changed an entry, it asks which to keep.
  • Any class with papers can have draft feedback and a notebook: they come from the class’s data.

Feedback on your WRIT 101 drafts

Sun, Oct 25, 2026 · build 2026.10.25.1
  • WRIT 101 has a Draft feedback page (in the sidebar under Study, and on the dashboard when a project draft is due next). Paste a draft of Project 1, 2 or 3 or the portfolio essay, or open a .docx, .odt or .txt file, and Bo reads it like a writing tutor.
  • He marks the passages he comments on in your draft, names the two or three biggest revisions for that project’s goals with a question for each, says what already works, and gives you three next steps. You can tell him what you want feedback on and paste your peer review comments.
  • He never writes it for you: the words stay yours. Each review is kept as a version of the project, so the next one says what changed since last time, and you can delete them any time.
  • Dr. Hall can switch draft feedback off for the class. A review uses a few credits.

Calls with Bo that hold up

Tue, Oct 20, 2026 · build 2026.10.20.2
  • Bo no longer stops and starts again mid-sentence: the app doesn’t cut him off to correct the math any more (he gets the check for his next turn instead), he answers one thing at a time, and a short “mm-hmm” or a noise doesn’t make him start over.
  • He understands you better: what you say is written down in your language with your class’s words as hints, and when he didn’t catch something he asks you to say it again instead of guessing.
  • No more “I ran into an error”: when a page or a drawing doesn’t work out, he fixes it quietly and carries on.
  • A dropped connection no longer ends the call: it says “reconnecting…” and comes back as the same call, with your board and the clock, and Bo picks up where you were. Only a whole minute without a connection ends it.
  • New in the call’s settings: “I’m wearing headphones” and “My speakers echo” (your mic stays closed while he talks, so he can’t interrupt himself; tap him to stop him).

Or pick Coach Bo, Detective Bo, Zen Bo, or Buddy Bo (the Bo you knew). Each has his own manner, voice and look; change him in Settings, at the top of Ask Bo, or in a call’s settings.
