3.2 Meiosis and life cycles

Study notes for Meiosis and life cycles in BIOB 160 Principles of Living Systems at Montana State University: the big ideas, key formulas, a worked example, common mistakes and an exam tip. The same topic has endless practice questions and flashcards in Mathub.

Big ideas

  • Homologous chromosomes carry the same genes (one from each parent). Diploid (2n) cells have two sets; haploid (n) gametes have one. Humans have 22 pairs of autosomes plus the sex chromosomes.
  • Meiosis I separates homologs (reduces 2n to n): in prophase I homologs pair (synapsis) and exchange segments by crossing over at chiasmata; in anaphase I homologs move apart while sister chromatids stay together. Meiosis II separates sister chromatids, like mitosis. One cell becomes four genetically different haploid cells.
  • Life cycles: animals make gametes by meiosis; plants and some algae show alternation of generations, with a multicellular diploid sporophyte making haploid spores by meiosis, which grow into a multicellular haploid gametophyte that makes gametes by mitosis; fungi and many protists have a haploid adult and a diploid zygote only.
  • Three sources of genetic variation (the “meiotic superpowers”): independent assortment of homologs (2ⁿ combinations; 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million in humans), crossing over, and random fertilization (about 70 trillion zygote combinations before crossing over).
  • Errors: nondisjunction (homologs or sisters fail to separate) produces aneuploid gametes, as in trisomy 21.

Key formulas

Combinations from independent assortment
$$2^n \ \text{(n = haploid number)}$$

Worked example

An organism has 2n = 6. How many chromosome combinations can its gametes have from independent assortment alone? How many chromosomes are in each gamete?
Show the solution
The haploid number is n = 3, so 2³ = 8 combinations. Each gamete has 3 chromosomes.

Common mistakes

  • Saying sister chromatids separate in meiosis I. Homologs separate in meiosis I; sisters separate in meiosis II.
  • Using 2n instead of n in the 2ⁿ formula.
  • Thinking the gametophyte makes gametes by meiosis. In alternation of generations, spores come from meiosis and gametes from mitosis.
Exam tipReading: Campbell ch. 13 (pp. 254–268) and p. 831 on alternation of generations. Draw meiosis for 2n = 4 once with colors; it answers most questions.
