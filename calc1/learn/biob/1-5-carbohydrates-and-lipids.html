1.5 Carbohydrates and lipids

Study notes for Carbohydrates and lipids in BIOB 160 Principles of Living Systems at Montana State University: the big ideas, a worked example, common mistakes and an exam tip. The same topic has endless practice questions and flashcards in Mathub.

Big ideas

  • Polymers are built by dehydration synthesis (removing a water molecule for each bond) and broken by hydrolysis (adding water).
  • Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose) join by glycosidic linkages into disaccharides (sucrose = glucose + fructose; lactose = glucose + galactose; maltose = glucose + glucose) and polysaccharides.
  • Storage polysaccharides: starch in plants and glycogen (highly branched) in animals, both α-glucose. Structural: cellulose (β-glucose, which most animals cannot digest) in plant cell walls and chitin in arthropod exoskeletons and fungal walls.
  • Fats (triglycerides) are glycerol plus three fatty acids joined by ester linkages. Saturated fatty acids have no C=C double bonds and pack tightly (solid at room temperature); unsaturated ones have cis double bonds that kink the chain (liquid oils). Trans fats are harmful.
  • Phospholipids have a hydrophilic phosphate head and two hydrophobic tails, so they form bilayers. Steroids have four fused rings; cholesterol sits in animal membranes and is the starting point for sex hormones. Lipids are not true polymers.

Worked example

How many water molecules are released when 6 glucose molecules are joined into one straight chain? What reaction breaks it down again?
Show the solution
Six units joined in a chain have 5 bonds, so 5 water molecules are released by dehydration synthesis. Hydrolysis (adding 5 water molecules) breaks it back into glucose.

Common mistakes

  • Calling lipids polymers. They are not built from repeating monomers.
  • Thinking starch and cellulose differ in their monomer. Both are glucose; the α vs β linkage makes the difference.
  • Mixing up saturated (no double bonds) and unsaturated (one or more double bonds).
Exam tipReading: Campbell 5.1–5.3 (pp. 66–78). A chain of n monomers releases n − 1 water molecules; this counting question is common.
