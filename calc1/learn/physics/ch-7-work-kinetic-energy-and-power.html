Ch 7 Work, kinetic energy, and power

Study notes for Work, kinetic energy, and power in PHSX 220 Physics I (w/ Calculus) at Montana State University: the big ideas, key formulas, a worked example, common mistakes and an exam tip. The same topic has endless practice questions and flashcards in MatHub.

Big ideas

  • Work by a constant force: $W = \vec F\cdot\vec d = Fd\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is between the force and the displacement. Forces perpendicular to motion (normal force, centripetal tension) do zero work; friction on a sliding object does negative work.
  • Variable force: $W = \int F_x\,dx$ = area under the $F$–$x$ graph. A spring stretched from 0 to $x$ stores $\tfrac12 kx^2$.
  • Work–energy theorem: $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = \tfrac12 mv_f^2 - \tfrac12 mv_i^2$. It replaces kinematics when you know forces and distances but do not care about time.
  • Power is the rate of doing work: $P = W/t = \vec F\cdot\vec v$. Units: watt; $1\ \text{hp} = 746$ W. Lifting at constant speed: $P = mgv$.

Key formulas

Work
$$W = Fd\cos\theta = \vec F\cdot\vec d,\qquad W = \int F_x\,dx$$
Kinetic energy and W–E theorem
$$K = \tfrac12 mv^2,\qquad W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K$$
Power
$$P = \frac{W}{t} = Fv\cos\theta$$

Worked example

A 50 kg sled at 4.0 m/s slides to rest on snow with $\mu_k = 0.10$. How far does it go? Use energy.
Show the solution
Only friction does work: $W = -\mu_k mg\,d = \Delta K = 0 - \tfrac12(50)(16) = -400$ J. So $d = 400/(0.10\cdot50\cdot9.8) = 8.16$ m.

Common mistakes

  • Using the angle between the force and the vertical instead of the displacement.
  • Counting the normal force as doing work on a block sliding on a surface.
  • Forgetting that KE depends on speed squared: doubling speed quadruples KE and stopping distance.
Exam tipIf a problem gives forces and distances and asks for speed (or vice versa) with no mention of time, use the work-energy theorem.
